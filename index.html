Credit Risk Modeling

Expected Loss (EL)

$$\text{EL} = \text{PD} \times \text{LGD} \times \text{EAD} = \text{PD} \times (1 - R) \times \text{EAD}$$
📐 Parameter Anatomy
  • $\text{PD}$ (Probability of Default): Probability that counterparty defaults over a specified horizon (typically 12 months), bounded in $[0, 1]$.
  • $\text{LGD}$ (Loss Given Default): Fraction of total exposure lost upon default, defined as $1 - R$, where $R$ is the recovery rate.
  • $\text{EAD}$ (Exposure at Default): Total outstanding dollar/euro claim at default, including drawn loans and converted credit lines.
  • $\text{EL}$: Statistical mean monetary loss expected in a portfolio over a normal business cycle.
💡 Trading & Economic Intuition
  • Cost of Doing Business: Expected Loss is NOT a tail risk. It is a predictable operating cost that must be priced into the loan margin or corporate credit spread.
  • EL vs Unexpected Loss (UL): EL is covered through loan loss reserves / provisions. Unexpected Loss (the volatility/tail of losses) requires equity capital.
  • Pre-default vs Post-default: PD is influenced by borrower leverage, volatility, and macro cycle. LGD is determined post-default by collateral quality, seniority, and bankruptcy jurisdiction.
🎯 CFA & Desk Application
  • Standard Recovery Benchmark: In corporate credit trading, senior unsecured debt typically assumes $R = 40\%$ ($\text{LGD} = 60\%$). Subordinated debt assumes $R \approx 15\text{--}20\%$.
  • IFRS 9 / CECL Staging: Stage 1 (performing loans) reserves 1-year EL. Stage 2 (significant credit deterioration) and Stage 3 (default) require Lifetime EL provisions.
  • Basel AIRB Capital: Regulatory capital requirements ($K$) depend on the Vasicek Gaussian copula formula, driving the Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA).
Intensity-Based Modeling

Survival Probability & Hazard Rates

$$S(t) = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \lambda(u)\,du\right) \xrightarrow{\lambda = \text{const}} S(t) = e^{-\lambda t}$$
📐 Parameter Anatomy
  • $\lambda(t)$ (Hazard Rate / Intensity): Instantaneous conditional probability rate of default at time $t$, given survival up to $t$: $\lambda(t) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\mathbb{P}(t < \tau \le t + \Delta t \mid \tau > t)}{\Delta t}$.
  • $S(t)$ (Survival Function): Probability that default time $\tau > t$. Monotonically non-increasing with $S(0) = 1$ and $\lim_{t \to \infty} S(t) = 0$.
  • $F(t) = 1 - S(t)$ (Cumulative PD): Unconditional probability of defaulting between time $0$ and $t$.
  • $f(t) = -S'(t) = \lambda(t) S(t)$: Marginal default probability density function.
💡 Trading & Economic Intuition
  • Poisson Arrival Process: Under constant $\lambda$, default arrives as a first jump of a Poisson process. The expected default time is $\mathbb{E}[\tau] = 1/\lambda$.
  • Compounding Survival: Multi-year survival is the product of annual survival rates: $S(T) = \prod_{i=1}^T (1 - \text{condPD}_i)$.
  • Curve Term Structure: In benign markets, credit curves slope upward ($\lambda_{5\text{Y}} > \lambda_{1\text{Y}}$) due to long-term uncertainty. In distress, $\lambda_{1\text{Y}}$ spikes, inverting the hazard curve.
🎯 CFA & Desk Application
  • Bootstrapping CDS Curves: Desk pricers discretize $\lambda(t)$ as piecewise-constant across benchmark maturities (1Y, 2Y, 3Y, 5Y, 7Y, 10Y) to fit quoted CDS spreads.
  • Mental Approximation: For small $\lambda t$, $S(t) \approx 1 - \lambda t$. Over 1 year, $S(1) \approx 1 - \lambda$, so the 1-year default probability is approximately $\lambda$.
  • Unconditional vs Conditional: Unconditional PD in year 3 is $S(2) - S(3) = e^{-2\lambda} - e^{-3\lambda}$, which is always lower than the conditional hazard rate $\lambda$.
Market Quoting & Quick Sizing

CDS–PD Fundamental Link

$$S_{\text{CDS}} \approx \lambda \times (1 - R) \iff \text{PD}_{1\text{Y}} \approx \frac{S_{\text{CDS}}}{1 - R}$$
📐 Parameter Anatomy
  • $S_{\text{CDS}}$: Par annual CDS spread in basis points or decimal form (e.g. $150\text{ bps} = 0.0150$).
  • $\lambda$: Annual risk-neutral default intensity (implied hazard rate).
  • $R$: Assumed recovery rate upon default ($0.40$ standard for senior unsecured corporates).
  • $1 - R$: Loss Given Default fraction ($0.60$ standard).
💡 Trading & Economic Intuition
  • No-Arbitrage Heuristic: Protection buyer pays $S$ per year. In default, seller pays $1 - R$. Equating expected cashflows over 1 year gives $S \approx \text{PD} \times (1 - R)$.
  • Desk Rule of Thumb: To find implied 1-year default probability, divide the CDS spread by $0.60$ (or multiply spread by $1.67$). Example: $300\text{ bps CDS} \implies \text{PD} \approx 300 / 0.60 = 5.0\%$.
  • Spread Sensitivity to Recovery: If market consensus lowers recovery from $40\%$ to $20\%$, the implied PD required to justify the same 300 bps spread falls to $300 / 0.80 = 3.75\%$.
🎯 CFA & Desk Application
  • Risk-Neutral ($\mathbb{Q}$) vs Physical ($\mathbb{P}$): $\text{PD}_{\mathbb{Q}}$ implied from CDS is substantially higher than historical rating agency default frequencies $\text{PD}_{\mathbb{P}}$ because CDS spreads price credit risk premia and jump-to-default risk.
  • Cash-CDS Basis: If a cash bond trades at a spread over swap $s_{\text{bond}}$ and CDS trades at $S_{\text{CDS}}$, Basis $= S_{\text{CDS}} - s_{\text{bond}}$. A negative basis allows buying the bond and buying CDS protection for an arbitrage carry.
  • High-Yield Approximation Limit: For deeply distressed names ($S > 1{,}000\text{ bps}$), the linear approximation breaks down; full numerical discounting is required.
Arbitrage-Free Pricing

CDS Fair Value & Upfront Payment

$$\text{PV}(\text{Protection Leg}) = \text{PV}(\text{Premium Leg}) \implies \text{Upfront} = (S_{\text{par}} - C) \times \text{RPV01}$$
📐 Parameter Anatomy
  • $\text{PV}(\text{Prot}) = (1 - R) \int_0^T D(0, t) (-dS(t))$: Present value of expected default compensation paid by protection seller.
  • $\text{PV}(\text{Prem}) = C \sum_{i=1}^N \Delta t_i D(0, t_i) S(t_i) + \text{Accrual}$: Present value of quarterly coupon payments made by protection buyer.
  • $S_{\text{par}}$: Breakeven par spread where upfront value is zero ($\text{PV}(\text{Prot}) = \text{PV}(\text{Prem})$).
  • $C$: Standard running contract coupon (100 bps for IG, 500 bps for HY).
💡 Trading & Economic Intuition
  • Big Bang Standardized Mechanics: Pre-2009, CDS traded at par spread without upfronts. Post-Big Bang, all contracts trade with fixed coupons ($100$ or $500$ bps) plus an upfront payment to settle the present value difference.
  • Buyer vs Seller Upfront: If $S_{\text{par}} > C$, protection buyer pays upfront cash to seller. If $S_{\text{par}} < C$, protection seller pays upfront cash (points rebate) to buyer.
  • MtM Closeout: The unwind value of an existing CDS position is the upfront difference between the original trade spread and current market par spread.
🎯 CFA & Desk Application
  • Clean vs Dirty Upfront: Quoted upfront is "clean" (excludes accrued premium since last roll). Settled upfront is "dirty" (includes accrued interest paid to the seller).
  • Standard IMM Roll Dates: Standard CDS contracts roll on the 20th of March, June, September, and December (IMM roll dates), standardizing liquidity.
  • ISDA Standard Model: The global standard model implemented in Bloomberg's CDSW uses standardized interest rate curves and piecewise constant hazard rates.
Risk & Sensitivity

Risky Duration & RPV01 (Spread01)

$$\text{RPV01} = \sum_{i=1}^N \Delta t_i \, D(0, t_i) \, S(t_i) + \text{Accrual on Default} \approx \frac{1 - e^{-(r + \lambda)T}}{r + \lambda}$$
📐 Parameter Anatomy
  • $\text{RPV01}$ (Risky PV01): Present value of a 1 basis point ($0.0001$) annuity paid quarterly until maturity or counterparty default, whichever comes first.
  • $D(0, t_i)$: Risk-free discount factor from valuation date to coupon payment date $t_i$.
  • $S(t_i)$: Survival probability of the reference entity to coupon date $t_i$.
  • $\Delta t_i$: Day count fraction (ACT/360 standard in corporate CDS markets).
💡 Trading & Economic Intuition
  • Credit Spread DV01: RPV01 is the credit desk's equivalent to bond modified duration. A 1 bp widening in credit spread changes position value by: $\Delta \text{MTM} = \text{Notional} \times \text{RPV01} \times \Delta S$.
  • Why Credit Dampens Duration: Risky duration is always strictly LESS than risk-free duration ($\text{RPV01} < \text{PV01}_{\text{risk-free}}$) because future cashflows are discounted twice: by the interest rate $r$ AND by the default hazard rate $\lambda$.
  • Spread Convexity: When credit deteriorates ($\lambda$ surges), RPV01 collapses rapidly. Protection buyers enjoy positive spread convexity (gains accelerate as spreads widen).
🎯 CFA & Desk Application
  • Curve Hedging / Sizing: In a 5Y vs 10Y CDS curve steepener trade, desks balance positions by $\text{RPV01}$ rather than face notional: $\text{Notional}_{5\text{Y}} \times \text{RPV01}_{5\text{Y}} = \text{Notional}_{10\text{Y}} \times \text{RPV01}_{10\text{Y}}$.
  • Cash vs Synthetic Hedging: To hedge a corporate bond portfolio with CDS, the hedge ratio is: $\beta = \frac{\text{Spread Duration}_{\text{bond}} \times \text{MV}_{\text{bond}}}{\text{RPV01}_{\text{CDS}} \times \text{Notional}_{\text{CDS}}}$.
  • Typical Values: A standard 5-year investment grade CDS has an RPV01 around $4.2\text{--}4.6$. A distressed HY name with high default probability may have an RPV01 of only $2.5\text{--}3.0$.
