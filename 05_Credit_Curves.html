The Term Structure of Credit Spreads

Pillars, market liquidity, and canonical curve topologies

Just like risk-free government curves, credit default swap spreads vary across tenors ($1\text{Y}, 2\text{Y}, 3\text{Y}, 5\text{Y}, 7\text{Y}, 10\text{Y}, 30\text{Y}$). The 5-year tenor is the undisputed global benchmark, representing over $70\text{--}75\%$ of all single-name CDS liquidity.

1. Upward Sloping

Normal / IG Benchmark

Healthy corporate issuers (IG) have negligible short-term default risk. Uncertainty, leverage creep, and rating migration risk increase with time.

2. Inverted Curve

Distressed / Restructuring

High near-term refinancing cliff or liquidity squeeze ($S_{1Y} \gg S_{5Y}$). If the company survives 1–2 years, post-restructuring survival chances brighten.

3. Humped Curve

Maturity Wall Risk

Specific medium-term debt maturity (e.g. 3Y bond maturity wall). Spreads peak around the bullet refinancing horizon, then subside for longer tenors.

4. Flat Curve

Stationary Hazard Rate

Constant hazard intensity $\lambda(t) = \bar{\lambda}$. Often observed in mature High Yield credits where default intensity is elevated but stationary.

💡
Desks Rule of Thumb: When an investment grade curve inverts ($1\text{Y CDS} > 5\text{Y CDS}$), it is the strongest possible market signal of impending distress. Hedge funds look to buy short-dated protection or short the equity via put options.

Piecewise Constant Hazard Rate Bootstrapping

Exact mathematical calibration of default intensities from quoted market CDS spreads

Because CDS contracts are quoted at discrete market pillars ($T_1 = 1\text{Y}, T_2 = 2\text{Y}, \dots, T_M = 10\text{Y}$), quantitative desks assume the hazard rate $\lambda(t)$ is piecewise constant over each interval $[T_{k-1}, T_k]$.

Piecewise Hazard Intensity & Survival Functions
$$\lambda(t) = \lambda_k \quad \text{for } t \in (T_{k-1}, T_k]$$ $$S(t) = S(T_{k-1}) \cdot \exp\left(-\lambda_k (t - T_{k-1})\right) \quad \text{for } t \in (T_{k-1}, T_k]$$ $$S(T_k) = \prod_{j=1}^k e^{-\lambda_j (T_j - T_{j-1})} = \exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^k \lambda_j \Delta T_j\right)$$
Step 1: First Pillar Calibration ($T_1$)

For the 1-year CDS trading at par spread $S_1$, there is only one unknown hazard rate $\lambda_1$:

$$S_1 \cdot \text{RPV01}_1(\lambda_1) = \text{ProtLeg}_1(\lambda_1)$$ $$\lambda_1 \approx \frac{S_1}{1 - R}$$
Step 2: Second Pillar Calibration ($T_2$)

With $\lambda_1$ already known, cash flows over $[0, T_1]$ are fixed. We solve for $\lambda_2$ such that the 2-year CDS reprices at par spread $S_2$:

$$S_2 \left[\text{RPV01}_{0 \to 1} + \text{RPV01}_{1 \to 2}(\lambda_2)\right] = \text{Prot}_{0 \to 1} + \text{Prot}_{1 \to 2}(\lambda_2)$$
Step 3: Recursive Induction ($T_k$)

Repeat inductively for each subsequent pillar $T_k \in \{3\text{Y}, 5\text{Y}, 7\text{Y}, 10\text{Y}\}$. Each step is a 1-dimensional root-finding problem that is strictly monotonic and guaranteed to have a unique solution.

⚡

Why Desks Use Piecewise Constant (ISDA Standard) Instead of Splines

Local determinism vs oscillations and negative forward hazard rates

✓ Piecewise Constant Intensity (ISDA Model)
  • Local Independence: Changing the 5Y quoted spread only alters $\lambda$ for $t > 3\text{Y}$; short-dated hazard rates ($\lambda_1, \lambda_2$) remain 100% untouched.
  • Positivity Guarantee: As long as the quoted term structure does not imply negative forward credit risk, $\lambda_k > 0$.
  • Market Standard: The ISDA Standard Model used for daily Big Bang upfront settlement strictly mandates piecewise constant hazard rates.
✗ Cubic Spline Interpolation Pitfalls
  • Global Feedback (Runge's Phenomenon): Adjusting the 10Y CDS quote ripples backward and changes the 1Y forward hazard rate.
  • Negative Forward Hazards: High polynomial curvature can easily cause instantaneous hazard rates to dip below zero ($\lambda(t) < 0$), creating negative default probabilities.
  • Arb Opportunities: Unstable forward hazard curves produce non-monotonic survival probabilities ($S(t_2) > S(t_1)$).

Curve Trading: Steepeners, Flatteners & CS01 Neutrality

Relative value positioning along the credit curve without taking directional market spread risk

Bull / Bear Steepener

Curve Steepener Trade

A curve steepener expresses the view that long-end spreads will widen relative to the short end ($S_{\text{long}} - S_{\text{short}} \uparrow$):

  • Buy Protection: Long-dated CDS (e.g. 5Y or 10Y)
  • Sell Protection: Short-dated CDS (e.g. 1Y or 2Y)
  • Wins When: Curve steepens (economic expansion, or long-term debt issuance surge).
Distress / Credit Event

Curve Flattener Trade

A curve flattener expresses the view that short-end spreads will widen faster than long-end spreads ($S_{\text{long}} - S_{\text{short}} \downarrow$):

  • Buy Protection: Short-dated CDS (e.g. 1Y or 2Y)
  • Sell Protection: Long-dated CDS (e.g. 5Y or 10Y)
  • Wins When: Curve flattens or inverts (liquidity crisis, debt maturity cliff).
The CS01 Neutral Sizing Rule
$$\text{CS01}_{\text{short}} \times \text{Notional}_{\text{short}} = \text{CS01}_{\text{long}} \times \text{Notional}_{\text{long}}$$ $$\text{Hedge Ratio } H = \frac{\text{Notional}_{\text{short}}}{\text{Notional}_{\text{long}}} = \frac{\text{RPV01}_{\text{long}}}{\text{RPV01}_{\text{short}}} > 1.0$$

Because short-dated CDS has a much smaller RPV01 ($\approx 1.8$ for 2Y vs $\approx 4.2$ for 5Y), you must trade significantly more notional on the short leg to eliminate parallel spread risk.

Credit Curve Roll-Down & Carry Mechanics

How steep curves generate structural P&L for protection sellers without spread changes

When a credit curve is upward-sloping and the market spread environment remains unchanged over time, an aging CDS position naturally "rolls down" the curve to a lower spread.

1. Running Coupon Carry

The quarterly cash coupon received by the protection seller:

$$\text{Coupon Carry} = \text{Notional} \times C \times \Delta t$$
2. Roll-Down Capital Gain

As the 5Y contract becomes a 4Y contract, its par spread drops from $S_{5Y}$ to $S_{4Y}$:

$$\text{Roll P\&L} \approx \text{Notional} \times (S_{5Y} - S_{4Y}) \times \text{RPV01}_4$$
3. Break-Even Widening Cushion

Total carry + roll provides an insurance buffer against market spread widening:

$$\Delta S_{\text{breakeven}} = \frac{\text{Carry} + \text{Roll P\&L}}{\text{CS01}}$$

Credit Curves Interview Masterclass

8 high-frequency desk interview questions on curve shapes, bootstrapping, and relative value

1. Why does an inverted CDS curve signal immediate restructuring or default danger?
Curve Diagnosis ▼

Concise Answer: Under normal conditions, credit curves slope upward because long-term uncertainty is greater than near-term uncertainty.

An inversion ($1\text{Y CDS} > 5\text{Y CDS}$) indicates the market prices an acute, imminent liquidity crisis or debt maturity cliff within 12 months. If the issuer successfully survives or restructures this near-term bottleneck, its survival probability over the subsequent 4 years actually improves (the "conditional survival" effect). Hence, short-end protection is priced at an emergency premium.

2. Walk me through the step-by-step logic of bootstrapping piecewise constant hazard rates.
Bootstrapping ▼

Concise Answer:

  1. Start at $T_1$ (1Y): Calibrate $\lambda_1$ by setting $\text{PV}(\text{Prot}_1) = S_1 \times \text{RPV01}_1$. Since $\lambda(t) = \lambda_1$ for $t \in [0, 1]$, this yields a closed-form root $\lambda_1 \approx S_1 / (1 - R)$.
  2. Move to $T_2$ (2Y): Fix $\lambda_1$ on $[0, 1]$. The unknown is $\lambda_2$ on $(1, 2]$. Compute the 2Y premium and protection legs as the sum of interval 1 (known) and interval 2 (function of $\lambda_2$). Solve for $\lambda_2$ such that $\text{NPV}_2 = 0$.
  3. Iterate recursively: Continue sequentially through $3\text{Y}, 5\text{Y}, 7\text{Y}, 10\text{Y}$. Because the equations are monotonic, a 1D Newton-Raphson or Brent's method converges within 4–5 iterations.
3. How do you size a CS01-neutral 2Y vs 5Y curve steepener, and which leg has larger notional?
Trade Sizing ▼

Concise Answer: To isolate curve twist from parallel spread moves, set $\text{CS01}_{\text{short}} = \text{CS01}_{\text{long}}$:

$$\text{Notional}_{\text{short}} \times \text{RPV01}_{\text{short}} = \text{Notional}_{\text{long}} \times \text{RPV01}_{\text{long}} \implies \text{Notional}_{\text{short}} = \text{Notional}_{\text{long}} \times \frac{\text{RPV01}_{\text{long}}}{\text{RPV01}_{\text{short}}}$$

Because $\text{RPV01}_{\text{long}}$ ($\approx 4.2$) is more than double $\text{RPV01}_{\text{short}}$ ($\approx 1.8$), the short-dated (2Y) leg requires more than 2x the notional of the long-dated (5Y) leg.

4. What is curve roll-down, and why is an upward-sloping credit curve profitable for protection sellers?
Roll-Down Carry ▼

Concise Answer: Roll-down is the capital appreciation an existing contract experiences as time passes along an unchanged upward-sloping curve.

If you sell 5Y protection at 160 bps and 4Y CDS is priced at 130 bps, after 1 year your position has 4 years remaining. Its market spread has tightened by 30 bps, giving the seller an upfront mark-to-market gain of $\Delta S \times \text{RPV01}_4$ in addition to collecting the 1-year coupon carry.

5. If 1Y CDS is 100 bps and 2Y CDS is 150 bps with $R = 40\%$, calculate the forward hazard rate for year 2.
Mental Math ▼

Concise Answer: Using the rule of thumb $\lambda \approx S / (1 - R)$ with $1 - R = 0.60$:

  • $\lambda_1 \approx 100 / 0.60 = 167\text{ bps}$ ($1.67\%$).
  • Average $\bar{\lambda}_2 \approx 150 / 0.60 = 250\text{ bps}$ ($2.50\%$).
  • Total cumulative hazard over 2 years: $2 \times 250 = 500\text{ bps}$.
  • Forward hazard for year 2: $\lambda_{1 \to 2} = 500 - 167 = \mathbf{333\text{ bps}}$ ($\mathbf{3.33\%}$).
6. What is the primary tail risk of a CS01-neutral curve steepener?
Tail Risk ▼

Concise Answer: Jump-to-Default (JTD) asymmetry.

In a steepener, you bought $10\text{M}$ 5Y protection and sold $\approx \$23\text{M}$ 2Y protection to be spread-neutral. If default strikes overnight, you collect $(1 - R) \times \$10\text{M}$ on your long leg, but must pay $(1 - R) \times \$23\text{M}$ on your short leg. You suffer an instant net loss of $(1 - R) \times \$13\text{M} \approx -\$7.8\text{M}$.

7. Why does the ISDA standard model use piecewise constant intensity rather than smooth splines?
Model Architecture ▼

Concise Answer:

  1. Locality of Hedging: A price shift in 5Y only affects $\lambda$ in $[3\text{Y}, 5\text{Y}]$, keeping earlier cashflows and hedges unchanged. Splines create nonlocal feedback across all tenors.
  2. Arbitrage Immunity: Splines often oscillate, resulting in negative forward hazard rates ($\lambda(t) < 0$) or increasing survival curves ($S(t_2) > S(t_1)$). Piecewise constant intensities are strictly non-negative if market spreads are arbitrage-free.
  3. Market Settlement: It is the globally mandated standard for clearinghouses (ICE, LCH) and Bloomberg CDSW calculations.
8. Explain how a credit curve butterfly trade (e.g. 2Y / 5Y / 10Y) is structured.
Butterfly Trades ▼

Concise Answer: A butterfly trades the curvature of the term structure:

$$\text{Butterfly Spread} = 2 \times S_{5Y} - (S_{2Y} + S_{10Y})$$

To execute a long butterfly (expecting 5Y spread to richen/widen relative to the wings):

  • Buy 5Y protection (the body).
  • Sell 2Y protection and 10Y protection (the wings).
  • Weight notionals such that both Total CS01 = 0 and Curve Slope Sensitivity = 0.
