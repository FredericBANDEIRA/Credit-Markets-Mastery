Credit Events & Auctions

The legal and market mechanics of credit defaults: ISDA Credit Events taxonomy, Restructuring clauses (XR, MR, MMR), Determinations Committee governance, and the two-stage auction settlement process.

📜

The ISDA Credit Events Taxonomy (2014 Definitions)

The 6 standard contractual default triggers governing OTC credit derivatives

Under the 2014 ISDA Credit Derivatives Definitions, a CDS contract pays out if and only if a designated Credit Event is officially declared by the ISDA Determinations Committee (DC) backed by Publicly Available Information (PAI).

1. Bankruptcy

Insolvency & Judicial Filings

Applies to all corporate entities. Triggered by voluntary or involuntary filing for Chapter 7 liquidation, Chapter 11 reorganization, receivership, or legal insolvency administration.

2. Failure to Pay

Missed Coupon or Principal

Failure to make a scheduled payment on one or more debt obligations after the contractual grace period expires (typically 30 calendar days or 3 business days), above the Payment Requirement threshold (typically $1,000,000 USD).

3. Restructuring

Distressed Debt Exchange

Reduction in coupon/principal, deferral of payment, change in priority/seniority, or mandatory currency redenomination agreed to in distress that binds all bondholders.

4. Governmental Intervention

Bail-in & Write-Downs

Added in the 2014 Definitions to address bank bail-ins (e.g. European BRRD rules). Triggered when a regulator mandates a reduction, cancellation, or debt-to-equity conversion of subordinated bank debt (e.g. Credit Suisse AT1s).

5. Repudiation / Moratorium

Sovereign Debt Freezes

Primarily applicable to Emerging Market and Sovereign CDS. Triggered when an authorized government authority disavows, repudiates, or declares a moratorium on debt service.

6. Obligation Acceleration

Cross-Default Acceleration

When an obligation becomes due and payable before its normal maturity date as a consequence of a covenant default on another debt issue (cross-acceleration).

💡
Publicly Available Information (PAI) Standard: A credit event requires confirmation published in at least two independent public sources (e.g. Bloomberg, Reuters, SEC 8-K filings, court dockets, or published news) before the ISDA DC will accept a question for deliberation.

Restructuring Conventions: XR, MR, and MMR

How regional bankruptcy cultures shape CDS deliverable obligation maturity limits

Under physical settlement, a protection buyer delivers defaulted debt in exchange for par. In a restructuring, long-dated bonds often trade at much lower dollar prices than short-dated debt due to duration discount. This created the notorious Cheapest-to-Deliver (CTD) distortion. ISDA introduced maturity caps to control this basis.

Restructuring Clause Market Standard Is Restructuring a Credit Event? Deliverable Maturity Limit Economic Rationale
XR (No Restructuring) North American Corporates (CDX NA IG/HY) NO None (Restructuring does not trigger CDS) US Chapter 11 is efficient; consensual debt exchanges should not trigger windfall payouts to CDS buyers.
MMR (Mod-Mod-R) European Corporates (iTraxx Europe / Xover) YES 60 Months (5 Years) for restructured debt; 30 months for other debt European legal systems lack Chapter 11 debtor-in-possession framework; out-of-court restructurings must trigger CDS, with 5Y delivery cap.
MR (Modified Restructuring) Historical US / Standard Sovereigns YES 30 Months (2.5 Years) post-restructuring Strict 30-month deliverable limit designed to prevent buyers from delivering 30Y distressed bonds into 1Y CDS contracts.
CR (Full Restructuring) Asian Corporates / Sovereigns YES Up to 30 Years (No maturity cap) Maximum CTD option value. Protection buyers deliver whichever bond has the lowest price regardless of remaining maturity.
Cheapest-to-Deliver (CTD) Option Value
$$\text{Payout} = \text{Notional} \times \left(100 - \min_{i \in \text{Deliverable}} P_i\right)$$

The wider the maturity basket, the lower the minimum price $\min(P_i)$, increasing the CTD option value embedded in the CDS spread: $S_{\text{CR}} > S_{\text{MMR}} > S_{\text{MR}} > S_{\text{XR}}$.

The ISDA Determinations Committee (DC) Governance

Composition, supermajority voting rules, and corporate succession split-up mechanics

Governance Framework

The 15-Member DC Structure

  • 10 Global Dealers: Leading sell-side investment banks (e.g. JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Barclays, Deutsche Bank).
  • 5 Buy-Side Members: Major asset managers, hedge funds, or pension managers (e.g. PIMCO, BlackRock, Citadel, Elliott).
  • Supermajority Threshold: Decisions require an 80% supermajority (12 out of 15 votes) to be final and binding.
  • External Review: If a vote fails to achieve 12 votes (e.g. 11-4 or 10-5), the question is referred to an independent 3-member External Review panel of legal experts.
Corporate Actions

Succession Event Rules

When an issuer undergoes a corporate split-up, merger, demerger, or transfer of debt:

  • Single Successor ($\ge 75\%$): If Entity A assumes $\ge 75\%$ of relevant debt, it becomes the 100% sole successor.
  • Notional Split ($25\%\text{--}75\%$): If multiple entities assume between $25\%$ and $75\%$ of debt, the CDS contract notional is divided equally among the successors!
  • Zero Successor ($< 25\%$): If no entity assumes $\ge 25\%$, the primary historical commercial operating company succeeds.

The Two-Stage Credit Event Auction Protocol

How Creditex and Tradeweb discover the market-wide recovery price ($R$)

Prior to the 2009 Big Bang Protocol, defaulted CDS settled via physical delivery of bonds. Because outstanding CDS notional frequently exceeded physical bonds by 5x to 10x (e.g. Delphi, Lehman Brothers), physical settlement caused severe short squeezes. The ISDA Two-Stage Auction was established to guarantee orderly cash settlement for the entire market.

Stage 1: Morning Session

Market Midpoint & Net Open Interest (NOI)

  • Dealer Two-Way Quotes: Participating dealers submit firm, executable bid/offer quotes within a maximum spread cap (e.g. 2 points).
  • Initial Market Midpoint (IMM): The average of the non-outlier dealer bids and offers establishes the IMM.
  • Physical Settlement Requests (PSRs): Dealers and clients submit requests to physically buy or sell bonds.
  • Net Open Interest (NOI): The sum of all buy vs sell PSRs. Typically, NOI is Net to Sell (protection buyers tend to deliver bonds).
Stage 2: Afternoon Session

Order Book Matching & Final Price

  • Limit Orders: Dealers and clients submit limit orders to purchase bonds (if NOI is to sell) or sell bonds (if NOI is to buy).
  • Order Book Matching: The auction administrator (Creditex) matches the Net Open Interest against the limit order book, starting from the highest bid down.
  • Final Price ($R$): The price of the last matched order that fully clears the Net Open Interest.
  • Cash Settlement: All CDS market participants settle at: $$\text{Payout} = (100 - R)\% \times \text{Notional}$$
⚠️
Dealer Quotation Penalty: To ensure dealers submit truthful quotes in Stage 1, a dealer who submits an off-market quote that is crossed by the IMM must pay a cash penalty (up to $50,000 per violation) to the auction pot!

Credit Events & Auctions Interview Masterclass

8 high-frequency desk interview questions on legal triggers, restructurings, and auctions

1. Walk me through the step-by-step two-stage ISDA credit event auction.
Auction Protocol ▼

Concise Answer: The auction establishes a binding market-wide recovery rate $R$ via two sessions:

  1. Stage 1 (Morning): Participating dealers submit firm, executable two-way bid/ask quotes within a mandatory spread tolerance. The midpoint of valid quotes forms the Initial Market Midpoint (IMM). Simultaneously, market participants submit Physical Settlement Requests (PSRs) to buy or sell physical deliverable bonds. The netting of buy vs sell PSRs defines the Net Open Interest (NOI).
  2. Stage 2 (Afternoon): Limit orders are submitted to absorb the NOI. If NOI is net to sell, limit bids are ranked from highest to lowest and matched against the NOI volume. The price of the marginal order that fully absorbs the NOI becomes the Final Price ($R$). All CDS contracts settle in cash at $(1 - R) \times \text{Notional}$.
2. What was the Credit Suisse AT1 wipeout and did it trigger Credit Suisse CDS?
Case Study ▼

Concise Answer: In March 2023, FINMA ordered the complete write-down of CHF 16B of Credit Suisse Additional Tier 1 (AT1) contingent convertible bonds during the UBS takeover.

  • Did Senior CDS Trigger? NO. Senior CDS references Senior Unsecured Debt, which was fully assumed by UBS without impairment.
  • Did Subordinated CDS Trigger? NO. The ISDA Determinations Committee ruled that the 2014 "Governmental Intervention" definition requires an action on an *Obligation* or *Deliverable Obligation*. The AT1 bonds did not qualify as deliverable obligations for the standard Subordinated CDS contract (which referenced Tier 2 subordinated debt, not AT1 equity-like hybrid capital). Hence, Subordinated CDS did NOT pay out!
3. Explain the difference between XR, MR, and MMR restructuring conventions.
Restructuring ▼

Concise Answer: The three clauses differ by whether Restructuring is an event and what bonds can be delivered:

  • XR (No Restructuring): Restructuring is excluded completely. Standard in North America (CDX IG/HY) because US Chapter 11 provides transparent court-supervised restructuring.
  • MR (Modified Restructuring): Restructuring is an event. Deliverable bonds are capped at 30 months post-event to curb cheapest-to-deliver distortion. Standard for US sovereign debt.
  • MMR (Modified Modified Restructuring): Restructuring is an event. Deliverable bonds are capped at 60 months (5 years) for restructured debt (30 months for other debt). Standard for European corporates (iTraxx Europe) where out-of-court restructuring is common.
4. How did the Delphi bankruptcy in 2005 illustrate the danger of physical settlement squeezes?
Historical Squeeze ▼

Concise Answer: When auto-parts supplier Delphi filed for Chapter 11 in October 2005:

  • Outstanding CDS protection sold on Delphi totaled approximately $28 Billion.
  • However, Delphi had only about $2 Billion of physical deliverable bonds outstanding.
  • Protection buyers rushed into the secondary cash market to acquire the scarce physical bonds needed to settle their CDS contracts. This desperate buying frenzy artificially bid up the defaulted bonds from 55 cents to over 72 cents!
  • This severe physical squeeze led directly to the creation of the cash-settled Credit Event Auction protocol in 2006.
5. What is a "Manufactured Default" (e.g. Hovnanian/GSO) and how did the 2019 NTCE rule stop it?
Market Manipulation ▼

Concise Answer:

  • The Hovnanian Trade (2018): Hedge fund GSO offered homebuilder Hovnanian favorable below-market financing on the strict condition that Hovnanian intentionally miss a tiny $1.04M interest payment to trigger a "Failure to Pay" on Hovnanian CDS where GSO held massive long protection!
  • The 2019 NTCE Amendment: ISDA enacted the Narrowly Tailored Credit Event (NTCE) rules. Under NTCE, a Failure to Pay is NOT recognized as a credit event if the failure results from an agreement between the issuer and a counterparty that does not reflect a bona fide deterioration in creditworthiness or financial condition.
6. How does the ISDA Determinations Committee handle a corporate spin-off where debt is split 60% / 40%?
Succession Events ▼

Concise Answer: Under the 2014 ISDA Definitions succession threshold rules:

Because neither entity assumes $\ge 75\%$ of the relevant debt, but both entities assume between $25\%$ and $75\%$ (Entity A has 60%, Entity B has 40%):

  • Both entities are declared joint successors.
  • The CDS contract is divided equally (50% / 50%) between Entity A and Entity B!
  • An original $10M CDS contract on the parent is replaced by a $5M contract referencing Entity A and a $5M contract referencing Entity B.
7. Why does a credit event auction set a single price across all deliverable obligations?
Cheapest to Deliver ▼

Concise Answer: Setting a single price solves the cheapest-to-deliver problem efficiently:

  • Under ISDA rules, any bond meeting the seniority and maturity criteria on the Deliverable Obligations List can be tendered by sellers into the auction.
  • Rational market participants tender the lowest-priced (cheapest-to-deliver) bonds. Therefore, the single auction clearing price naturally converges to the value of the cheapest deliverable bond.
  • Having a single price ensures that all CDS contracts cash-settle at an identical, transparent payout rate $(100 - R)\%$, eliminating dispute litigation across different counterparties.
8. What are the Payment Requirement and Grace Period conditions for a Failure to Pay?
Thresholds ▼

Concise Answer: Under standard 2014 ISDA Confirmation terms:

  • Payment Requirement: The missed amount must equal or exceed $1,000,000 USD (or equivalent in contract currency) for corporates, or $100,000 USD for micro-credits. Missed operational dust payments cannot trigger a CDS.
  • Grace Period Extension: If the underlying bond indenture contains a contractual grace period (typically 30 calendar days for coupons, 3 business days for principal), the credit event does NOT occur until the grace period has fully expired without payment.
