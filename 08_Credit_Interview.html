Credit Derivatives Master Formula Matrix

Comprehensive mathematical reference across all 8 modules

1. Expected Loss (EL) & Recovery
$$\text{EL} = \text{PD} \times \text{LGD} \times \text{EAD} = \text{PD} \times (1 - R) \times \text{EAD}$$

LGD is loss given default; $R$ is recovery rate (standard senior unsecured: 40%).

2. Fundamental Par CDS Rule of Thumb
$$S_{\text{par}} \approx \lambda(1 - R) \iff \lambda \approx \frac{S_{\text{par}}}{1 - R}$$

Exact under constant hazard rate $\lambda$ and flat risk-free interest rates.

3. Survival & Cumulative Default Functions
$$S(t) = \exp\left(-\int_0^t \lambda(u)\,du\right) \xrightarrow{\lambda = \text{const}} S(t) = e^{-\lambda t}$$ $$F(t) = 1 - S(t) = 1 - e^{-\lambda t}, \quad f(t) = \lambda e^{-\lambda t}$$
4. Cash-CDS Basis & Negative Basis Arb
$$\text{Basis} = S_{\text{CDS}} - Z_{\text{bond}} \approx S_{\text{CDS}} - \text{ASW}_{\text{bond}}$$

If Basis $< 0$: Buy bond + asset swap to floating + Buy CDS protection (locks in risk-free spread).

5. Risky PV01 (RPV01 / Spread01)
$$\text{RPV01} = \sum_{i=1}^N \Delta t_i D(0, t_i) S(t_i) + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^N \Delta t_i D(0, t_i) [S(t_{i-1}) - S(t_i)]$$ $$\text{Continuous Approx:} \quad \text{RPV01} \approx \frac{1 - e^{-(r + \lambda)T}}{r + \lambda}$$
6. CDS Upfront Cash Settlement
$$\text{Upfront (Clean)} = (S_{\text{par}} - C) \times \text{RPV01} \times \text{Notional}$$ $$\text{Dirty Upfront} = \text{Clean Upfront} + \text{Accrued Interest}$$
7. CS01-Neutral Curve Hedge Ratio
$$\text{Hedge Ratio } H = \frac{\text{Notional}_{\text{short}}}{\text{Notional}_{\text{long}}} = \frac{\text{RPV01}_{\text{long}}}{\text{RPV01}_{\text{short}}} > 1.0$$
8. Jump to Default (JTD)
$$\text{JTD (Buyer)} = \text{Notional} \times (1 - R) - \text{Dirty Upfront}$$ $$\text{JTD (Seller)} = -\text{Notional} \times (1 - R) + \text{Dirty Upfront}$$

12 Comprehensive Quantitative Interview Questions

Synthesizing fundamentals, spreads, mechanics, pricing, curves, indices, and auctions

1. Explain why CDS protection buyers enjoy positive spread convexity while bondholders face negative convexity.
Convexity▼

Concise Answer: For a CDS protection buyer, as spreads widen, mark-to-market gains grow, and maximum gain is $(1 - R)$ upon default; as spreads tighten to 0, maximum loss is strictly capped at the upfront/premium paid ($\partial^2 \text{NPV} / \partial S^2 > 0$). In contrast, callable corporate bondholders suffer negative convexity because the embedded call option truncates upside as yields drop.

2. Walk me through the difference between Z-spread, Par-Par Asset Swap Spread, and OAS.
Spread Taxonomy▼

Concise Answer:

  • Z-spread: Constant spread added to the risk-free spot yield curve to discount bond cash flows to current market price.
  • Par-Par ASW: The fixed coupon exchanged for Euribor/SOFR + spread under an interest rate swap where the investor buys the bond at par. Diverges from Z-spread when bond trades away from par due to upfront principal mismatch.
  • OAS (Option-Adjusted Spread): The Z-spread stripped of embedded options (call/put/prepayment) using a Monte Carlo short-rate lattice.
3. Derive the continuous-time no-arbitrage proof that $S = \lambda(1 - R)$.
Derivation▼

Concise Answer: Under constant risk-free rate $r$ and hazard intensity $\lambda$:

$\text{PV}(\text{Protection Leg}) = (1 - R) \int_0^T e^{-rt} \lambda e^{-\lambda t} dt = \lambda(1 - R) \int_0^T e^{-(r + \lambda)t} dt$.

$\text{PV}(\text{Premium Leg}) = S \int_0^T e^{-rt} e^{-\lambda t} dt = S \int_0^T e^{-(r + \lambda)t} dt = S \times \text{RPV01}$.

Equating $\text{PV}(\text{Prot}) = \text{PV}(\text{Prem})$ cancels the common integral $\text{RPV01}$, yielding exact equality: $\mathbf{S = \lambda(1 - R)}$.

4. Why is Risky PV01 strictly smaller than Risk-Free PV01?
RPV01▼

Concise Answer: Cashflows in Risky PV01 are discounted twice: first by the interest rate discount factor $D(0, t)$, and second by the survival probability $S(t) = e^{-\lambda t} \le 1$. In distressed names with large hazard rates, distant cashflows vanish, compressing RPV01 dramatically below risk-free duration.

5. Explain the mechanics of a Negative Basis Trade and why it rarely exists in modern markets.
Basis Trading▼

Concise Answer: When $S_{\text{CDS}} < Z_{\text{bond}}$, an arbitrageur buys the cash bond, asset-swaps it to floating rate, and buys CDS protection. This locks in a risk-free spread. Today, post-2008 Basel III leverage ratios and capital charges (LCR, SLR) make holding funded bond inventory expensive, so small negative bases persist without being fully arb'd away.

6. Why does a CS01-neutral 2s5s curve steepener carry massive negative Jump-to-Default (JTD)?
JTD Asymmetry▼

Concise Answer: Because $\text{RPV01}_5 \approx 4.25$ is more than double $\text{RPV01}_2 \approx 1.86$, CS01 neutrality requires selling $H \approx 2.28\text{x}$ notional on the 2Y leg against $1.0\text{x}$ on the 5Y leg. If default occurs overnight, the desk collects $(1 - R) \times \$10\text{M}$ but owes $(1 - R) \times \$22.8\text{M}$, suffering a massive net loss of $-\$7.68\text{M}$.

7. What is the economic intuition for why default correlation affects equity and senior tranches oppositely?
Correlation▼

Concise Answer: Correlation dictates portfolio loss variance. At low correlation, defaults are diversified; a steady number of defaults is guaranteed, wiping out the first-loss Equity [0-3%] tranche while leaving Senior [15-30%] pristine. At high correlation, firms default together or survive together; the chance of zero defaults rises (tightening equity spread), while the tail risk of joint collapse explodes (widening senior spread).

8. Walk me through the step-by-step two-stage ISDA credit event auction.
Auctions▼

Concise Answer: (1) Stage 1: Participating dealers submit two-way quotes establishing the Initial Market Midpoint (IMM). Market participants submit Physical Settlement Requests (PSRs) to buy/sell physical bonds, creating Net Open Interest (NOI). (2) Stage 2: Limit orders are submitted and matched against the NOI. The marginal order clearing the NOI sets the final recovery price $R$. All CDS settle at $(100 - R)\%$.

9. Why do European corporate CDS trade with MMR while US corporate CDS trade with XR?
Restructuring▼

Concise Answer: US corporate bankruptcy law has an efficient Chapter 11 mechanism where companies reorganize under court supervision; hence US market participants prefer XR (no restructuring) to avoid triggering CDS on consensual exchange offers. In Europe, fragmented legal regimes encourage out-of-court restructurings; European investors demand restructuring protection (MMR), with deliverable bonds capped at 60 months to limit CTD distortion.

10. What is index skew and what drives it to become positive or negative?
Index Skew▼

Concise Answer: $\text{Skew} = S_{\text{index}} - S_{\text{intrinsic}}$. Skew is positive (index rich) when macro hedgers flood into liquid index protection during market sell-offs. Skew is negative (index cheap) when investment banks hedge structured synthetic CDO/CLO tranches by selling index protection.

11. What is base correlation and why did it replace compound correlation in synthetic tranche modeling?
Copula Models▼

Concise Answer: Compound correlation solves for a correlation parameter for a specific bespoke tranche $[K_L, K_U]$. It failed because mezzanine tranches often yielded non-unique or nonexistent roots. Base correlation maps all tranches as standard equity loss layers $[0, K]$. Bespoke tranches $[K_L, K_U]$ are priced as the difference between two base tranches $[0, K_U] - [0, K_L]$, providing a monotonic, uniquely invertible curve.

12. Explain the Narrowly Tailored Credit Event (NTCE) amendment and the Hovnanian trade.
Market Integrity▼

Concise Answer: In 2018, Blackstone GSO offered homebuilder Hovnanian favorable financing in exchange for Hovnanian intentionally missing a tiny $1.04M interest payment to trigger Hovnanian CDS where GSO held massive protection. In 2019, ISDA adopted the NTCE rule: a Failure to Pay is disqualified if it results from a manufactured agreement that does not reflect a genuine credit deterioration of the borrower.
